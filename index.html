CONSTITUTIONAL EMERGENCY · 25 JUNE 1975 — 21 MARCH 1977
A black day in constitutional history

The Emergency.

On 25 June 1975 the Constitution was turned against itself. For twenty-one months, fundamental rights were suspended, the press was censored, and citizens were detained without trial. This is the record — and the correction that followed.

BLACK DAY
25 JUNE 1975 1975 — 1977
The twenty-one months

How the Republic was suspended.

  1. 25 June 1975

    The proclamation

    President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed signs an internal emergency under Article 352 on the advice of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi.

  2. 26 June 1975

    Rights suspended

    Fundamental rights are suspended and habeas corpus is effectively shut. Thousands are detained under MISA and the Defence of India Rules.

  3. 1975–76

    The press goes quiet

    Pre-censorship is imposed. Newspapers leave front pages blank in protest, and foreign correspondents are expelled.

  4. 28 April 1976

    ADM Jabalpur

    A 4:1 Supreme Court majority holds that detainees cannot enforce habeas corpus during the Emergency. Justice H. R. Khanna dissents.

  5. 1976

    The 42nd Amendment

    The “Mini Constitution” adds Socialist and Secular to the Preamble, curtails judicial review, and extends the Lok Sabha’s term.

  6. 21 March 1977

    The Republic answers

    The Emergency is lifted. The Janata Party wins the election, and the 44th Amendment reverses the damage in 1978.

The record

What was taken.

01

The trigger

Article 352 allowed a proclamation for “internal disturbance”. The 44th Amendment replaced that ground with “armed rebellion” and required written Cabinet advice.

Art. 352
02

Rights under arrest

Article 359 let the State suspend the enforcement of fundamental rights. The 44th Amendment made Articles 20 and 21 non-suspendable.

Art. 359
03

The silent press

Pre-censorship emptied the newspapers. No constitutional amendment restored the press; the courts and the press itself did.

Censorship
04

The Court divided

ADM Jabalpur was overruled in 2017. The right to move court for habeas corpus is now understood to survive even an emergency.

2017
05

The Mini Constitution

The 42nd Amendment tried to shrink judicial review and lengthen the House. Much of it was undone within two years.

42nd Amendment
06

The correction

The 44th Amendment rebuilt the safeguards around emergency power and restored the rights the Emergency had taken.

The lesson of 1975–77
The correction

What the Constitution learned.

The 44th Amendment (1978) rebuilt the safeguards around Article 352:

  • A proclamation needs the written advice of the Cabinet.
  • The ground changed from “internal disturbance” to “armed rebellion”.
  • Parliament must approve it by a special majority within one month.
  • Articles 20 and 21 — fairness and life — cannot be suspended.
  • The Lok Sabha’s term reverted to five years.
The promise

Never again.

The Emergency ended not because the institutions saved themselves, but because an election was held and the people answered. Remember the date. Defend the safeguards.
